The Algorithmic Front: AI in Modern Defence

How artificial intelligence is quietly reshaping military doctrine, reaction times, global force capabilities, and the high-stakes ethics of autonomous lethality.

By Yassin Xaviour • Published October 2026 • 8 min read • Strategic Dispatch

Artificial Intelligence has shifted from theoretical military research into operational doctrine across land, air, sea, and cyberspace. Modern forces deploy AI primarily across Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR), automated target generation, autonomous counter-measures, and predictive logistics.

The commercial and strategic momentum behind this transition is historic. The global military AI market is estimated at $12.2 billion and is on track to exceed $35.0 billion by 2035 at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 12.7% (Precedence Research).

What makes algorithmic systems transformative is not merely their cognitive breadth, but their capacity to operate at speeds unreachable by human operators—forever compressing the decision space between observation and engagement.

"The transition from human-directed violence to machine-speed latency represents the most profound doctrinal shift since the mechanization of warfare."

NATIONAL FOCUS: DEFENCE AI

India has rapidly accelerated its military AI doctrine from experimental prototyping into frontline deployment. Driven by dual-front geopolitical tensions across the Line of Actual Control (LAC) with China and western borders, the Indian Ministry of Defence (MoD) established the Defence AI Council (DAIC) chaired by the Defence Minister, alongside the Defence AI Project Agency (DAIPA).

Under the flagship initiative Project "AIDAI" (AI in Defence), India has categorized and deployed 75 prioritized defence AI capabilities. Each Defence Public Sector Undertaking (DPSU) has been mandated to earmark at least ₹100 Crore annually for dedicated AI research, scaling India’s domestic defense AI pipeline beyond ₹1,000+ Crore across startups, academia, and military laboratories.

⛰️ Northern Border Surveillance

Line of Actual Control (LAC): Deployment of AI-powered high-altitude thermal cameras, facial recognition, and automated intrusion detection systems developed by DRDO and domestic startups to maintain 24/7 unblinking watch along Ladakh and Arunachal Pradesh.

🌊 Maritime Acoustic Intelligence

Indian Ocean Region (IOR): Indian Navy partnered with DRDO’s NPOL to operationalize AI-driven passive sonar classification algorithms (Sonar-Net) on P-8I Neptune patrol aircraft to autonomously detect and categorize hostile submarine acoustic signatures.

✈️ Loyal Wingman & Swarm Drones

Air Combat & Swarms: HAL’s Combat Air Teaming System (CATS) with the "CATS Warrior" autonomous loyal wingman. The Indian Army has also inducted operational 100+ swarm drone systems from NewSpace Research & Technologies and ideaForge.

Strategic Partnerships: INDUS-X (India-U.S. Defence Acceleration) & Quad Critical Technology Workgroups. Key R&D Centers: DRDO CAIR & DYSL-AI (Young Scientist Labs).

The Strategic Trade-Off: Pros vs. Cons

Strategic Advantages (Pros)

  • Drastic Reduction in Reaction Times

    Decisive advantage in hypersonic defense, drone swarm interception, and electronic warfare where human reflexes are fatally slow.

  • Force Multiplier at Lower Attrition

    Unmanned autonomous systems preserve frontline military personnel lives while scaling patrol reach, reconnaissance, and strike capabilities.

  • Cognitive Load Relief for Commanders

    Filters out 95%+ of raw battlefield sensor noise so human commanders only evaluate high-probability, mission-critical tactical data.

Systemic Hazards & Risks (Cons)

  • Algorithmic Hallucination & Collateral Damage

    High error rates in unfamiliar sensor conditions, dense urban clutter, weather distortion, or deliberately spoofed adversarial inputs.

  • The "Black Box" Accountability Gap

    Lack of model explainability makes international humanitarian law (IHL) compliance, Geneva Conventions, and chain-of-command accountability difficult to govern.

  • Escalation Velocity (Hyper-War)

    Hyperspeed algorithmic engagements increase the likelihood of unintended conflict flashpoints before human diplomacy can intervene.

Editor’s Note: The Threshold of Autonomous Lethality

Warfare has crossed an irrevocable threshold. The historic doctrine of human-directed violence—governed by chain of command, political intent, and moral friction—is yielding to the relentless calculus of machine latency. As modern militaries deploy AI-driven target generation suites and autonomous interceptors, the debate over autonomous lethality has shifted from theoretical ethics to immediate operational reality.

The strategic imperative driving this transition is clear: speed. In contested battlespaces defined by hypersonic vectors, coordinated drone swarms, and aggressive electronic warfare, human reaction times are a fatal vulnerability. Relinquishing terminal engagement decisions to edge algorithms is no longer an experimental pursuit; it is rapidly becoming an operational necessity to ensure survivability.

Yet this tactical advantage introduces a profound moral and systemic hazard. An algorithm possesses neither conscience nor contextual restraint. It optimizes exclusively for statistical confidence within predefined objective functions. When a machine misclassifies a target in the fog of kinetic conflict, the moral and legal accountability framework collapses. Who answers for the error: the commander, the developer, or the model weights?

Moreover, compressing the sensor-to-shooter loop from minutes to milliseconds risks removing the vital friction that prevents uncontrolled escalation. Algorithmic interactions between opposing autonomous systems threaten to trigger conflict flashpoints before human oversight can intervene.

The path forward cannot rely on bans that peer competitors will simply disregard, nor on naive faith in flawless code. Defense leadership must enforce rigorous architectural boundaries: non-negotiable human-in-the-loop tripwires on strategic strike authorities, verifiable explainability standards for tactical AI, and resilient failsafes against spoofed inputs.

AI can identify patterns, process sensor streams, and accelerate tactical options at unprecedented scale. But the moral burden of lethality—the irreversible choice to extinguish human life—must remain an exclusively human responsibility. The moment we automate our conscience, we surrender the very doctrine we exist to defend.
